# Power budget and break-even: can the panel pay for its own movement?

EEE3035 Group 8, Active Solar Tracker. Generated by `power_budget.py` on 19 Nov 2026; every number in this file comes from the script (`power_budget_numbers.json` holds them unrounded). **This is a model. Nothing here has been measured on our hardware yet**: the values are datasheet figures, product-listing figures, cited third-party measurements, or assumptions marked as such. Section 8 lists the bench measurements for 26 Nov that replace them. Changed on 19 Nov 2026: the actuator is a DS-R001 hobby servo, power-gated by the firmware (it replaces the worm-gear motor, DRV8833 driver and limit switches assumed before), and the consumption sensor sits in the 5 V feed to the tracker.

## 1. Answer in brief

- **Break-even budget** (tracked minus fixed panel at the same tilt, PVGIS): the tracker may draw at most **0.70 Wh/day = 29 mW average in December**, 1.03 Wh/day (43 mW) in January, 1.46 Wh/day (61 mW) in November and 7.61 Wh/day (317 mW) in June. Year mean 4.44 Wh/day (185 mW).
- **(a) always awake, Wi-Fi on, servo powered**: 7.18 Wh/day (299 mW). Net loss in 9 months (Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec); -18 % over the year.
- **(b) data link as built**: 2.87 Wh/day (120 mW). Net loss in 5 months (Jan, Feb, Oct, Nov, Dec); +10 % over the year.
- **(c) light sleep, 15 min uploads**: 0.49 Wh/day (20 mW). Net gain in every month; +26 % over the year.
- **(d) light sleep, hourly uploads, LED cut**: 0.29 Wh/day (12 mW). Net gain in every month; +27 % over the year.
- **Design target: P_use <= 22.5 mW average (0.54 Wh/day)** at the consumption sensor (#3, in the 5 V feed). That is the December budget divided by 1.3, so the net gain stays positive in every month even if consumption is 30 % above the model.
- **The servo must be power-gated.** Left powered all day, its standby current alone (10-40 mA at 5 V, listings disagree) costs 1.2-4.8 Wh/day. That is 1.7 to 6.8 times the whole December tracking gain (0.70 Wh/day), before the MCU draws anything.
- **Power-gated, the servo is negligible:** about 4 mWh/day for 31 moves (worst case 29 mWh/day). The electronics that are on for 24 h then decide the result, not the movement. Gating only works if the servo holds the panel when it is not powered. That is not known yet (bench test M9).

## 2. Method

1. `net gain = (E_tracked - E_fixed - E_use) / E_fixed` (contract 6.2). Net gain >= 0 exactly when `E_use <= E_tracked - E_fixed`. The right-hand side is the **break-even budget**; divided by 24 h it is the maximum average tracker power.
2. `E_tracked` and `E_fixed` are the PVGIS 5.3 monthly means already in the repository (docs/energy/pvgis_Ed_per_kWp_guildford.json): inclined-axis tracker and fixed panel at the same tilt (39°, south), 14 % system loss, scaled to one 5.4 W FIT0330 panel.
3. `E_use` is built bottom-up: for each operating mode, current of each part x rail voltage / converter efficiency x time in that mode. All scenario figures are **at the 5 V feed to the tracker** (the power-bank output), which is where the consumption sensor (#3) sits since the wiring correction of 19 Nov, so it sees the whole tracker and nothing else (see flag F1). P_use is measured there, so the losses of the converter upstream (inside the power bank or the power manager) are outside P_use. 3.3 V loads go through the Nano's MP2322 buck converter (0.90 at normal load, 0.80 assumed at sleep currents). The servo is fed at 5 V from the same feed, after the sensor.
4. The servo is added as separate terms (section 4.1): its standby current if it stays powered, and the energy of the moves.
5. Cross-check: with the repository's own `E_use` assumptions the script reproduces `docs/energy/net_gain_by_month.md` (section 5).

Flags (things the model had to assume):

- **F1, where sensor #3 sits.** Settled on 19 Nov 2026: the consumption sensor is in the 5 V feed to the tracker, so its bus voltage (channel-1 field7, V_supply) is the supply-rail voltage, not a battery voltage. Losses upstream of that point (the power bank's converter, or the power manager's boost at about 0.86) are outside P_use. Section 6 shows what counting them would do.
- **F2, Nano ESP32 supply.** Arduino recommends 6-21 V on VIN (the cheat sheet also says keep VIN within 5-18 V), so 5 V on VIN is below the recommended range, and VBUS is not an input (it only outputs 5 V when the board is powered from USB). Feed the power manager's 5 V USB output into the Nano's USB-C port, or 3.3 V into the 3V3 pin only as Arduino describes (solder jumper), which also removes the MP2322 loss.
- **F3, BPX65 arc.** The repository does not say how many photodiodes there are or how they are read; 5 diodes reverse-biased from 3V3 are assumed. Used in photovoltaic mode (no bias) they draw nothing.
- **F4, servo.** The DS-R001 figures come from product listings that disagree (standby <= 10 mA, <= 20 mA or <= 40 mA). The energy of a move and the number of moves are assumptions. Whether the servo holds the panel when it is not powered is not known. Running current, move time and holding are bench items M6 to M9.
- **F5, panel-angle sensor.** With a servo the panel angle is the commanded angle unless a separate sensor is fitted; there are no limit switches. A 10 kΩ potentiometer across 3.3 V would add 0.33 mA (about 0.03 Wh/day): power it from a GPIO during the wake window.
- **F6, board sleep currents** come from forum users, not from Arduino. They vary by a factor of two.
- **F7, power bank.** Many power banks switch off when the load falls below roughly 50-100 mA. Light sleep draws only a few mA, so the two clash. Use a bank with a low-current (always-on) mode, or the Waveshare Solar Power Manager with the 18650 cell from the BOM. Bench item M13.
- **F8, power sensors.** The block diagram and the ordered BOM disagree: three INA226 or one four-channel INA219 board. The firmware detects which. The model uses the INA226 currents; an INA219 draws more (about 0.7 mA each, 1 mA maximum; to be checked against its datasheet) and averages over at most 68 ms, so a 0.4 s servo move can fall between two 1 Hz reads.

### 4.1 Servo energy

The actuator is a DS-R001 hobby servo fed at 5 V. Two cases. Both are models until M6 to M9 are measured.

**(a) Servo always powered.** The servo draws its standby current for 24 h whether it moves or not. The listings give three different upper limits, so all three are shown.

| Standby current at 5 V | Power (mW) | **Wh/day** | Share of the December budget | Months with a net loss from the servo alone | (b) total | (c) total | (d) total | Months with a net loss, (c) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 mA | 50 | **1.2** | 171 % | Jan, Dec | 4.07 | 1.69 | 1.49 | Jan, Nov, Dec |
| 20 mA | 100 | **2.4** | 342 % | Jan, Feb, Nov, Dec | 5.27 | 2.89 | 2.69 | Jan, Feb, Oct, Nov, Dec |
| 40 mA | 200 | **4.8** | 684 % | Jan, Feb, Mar, Oct, Nov, Dec | 7.67 | 5.29 | 5.09 | Jan, Feb, Mar, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec |

The December tracking gain is 0.70 Wh/day. An ungated servo costs 1.2 to 4.8 Wh/day on its own, so it wipes out the December gain at every listed standby figure, even with the best sleep scheme for the electronics. The totals are the scenario totals of section 4 plus the standby energy.

**(b) Servo power-gated.** A high-side P-MOSFET, switched from a GPIO, feeds the servo only around a move. Assumptions: the control code moves when the pointing error exceeds the 5° dead band, about one move per 20 min of daylight (sun at 15°/h), plus one return move after dusk; a small move takes 0.2 J (about 100 mA mean for 0.4 s at 5 V), worst 2 J; the return move is counted at 2 J; the servo is powered for 1 s per move (standby 20 mA for the rest of that second) and the MCU stays awake for it; gate leakage about 1 µA (0.12 mWh/day, negligible).

| Daylight | Moves/day | Servo powered (s/day) | Moves (J) | Standby in the powered window (J) | MCU awake for the moves (J) | **Total, typical (mWh/day)** | **Total, worst move (mWh/day)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 h | 25 | 25 | 6.8 | 1.5 | 2.7 | **3.0** | **15.0** |
| 10 h | 31 | 31 | 8.0 | 1.9 | 3.3 | **3.7** | **18.7** |
| 16 h | 49 | 49 | 11.6 | 2.9 | 5.2 | **5.5** | **29.5** |

Gated, the servo costs a few mWh/day: 0.4 % of the December budget (typical, 8 h of daylight), 2.1 % with the worst move figure. Gating cuts the servo's energy by a factor of about 300 to 1300. The 5° dead band costs at most 0.13 % of the beam (error between 0 and 5°), so moving more often buys nothing.

Three things can spoil case (b):

- **Holding.** The servo has plastic spur gears, which are not self-locking (the worm gear was), and a clutch that slips under external force. If the panel moves when the servo is not powered (wind, its own weight), gating between moves is not possible as it stands and case (a) applies. Bench test M9.
- **A blocked servo.** At stall it draws about 1.4 A at 5 V (7 W) until its own protection cuts the motor after 5 s: about 35 J per event, as much as 3 typical days of moves. The firmware should switch the gate off sooner and report fault 8.
- **Hunting.** Every extra move costs the powered window as well as the move. Keep the dead band and do not move in state HLD.

- **Reference panel flat instead of tilted.** The budget rises to 4.21 Wh/day in December (176 mW) and 6.59 in June, so even scenario (b) shows a gain in 12 of 12 months. Most of that is the tilt, not the tracking (Nov: flat 4.1, tilted 8.6, tracked 10.1 Wh/day), so it must not be reported as tracking gain.
- **Consumption ±30 %.** (d) stays positive in every month at +30 % (December headroom +143 %). (c) has +44 % December headroom, which survives +30 %.
- **Servo left powered.** See section 4.1: (c) rises from 0.49 to 1.69-5.29 Wh/day and shows a net loss in 3 to 7 months. No other single assumption moves the result as much.
- **Upstream converter.** P_use is measured at the 5 V feed, so the loss of the converter that makes the 5 V is outside P_use. If the group decides to count it, every figure is 1/0.86 = 1.16 times larger (86 % is the Solar Power Manager's figure; a power bank is assumed to be similar). The power manager's own quiescent current (< 2 mA, at most 0.18 Wh/day) is not charged to the tracker either, because a fixed panel with a battery needs the same module; with both counted, December is (c) -0.04 Wh/day; (d) +0.19 Wh/day, so measure the real figures (M11).
- **Power bank switching off.** Many power banks switch off when the load falls below roughly 50-100 mA. Scenarios (c) and (d) draw a few mA, so an ordinary bank may cut the supply (flag F7, bench item M13). Adding a dummy load to keep it on would cost far more than the December budget (50 mA at 5 V is 6 Wh/day).
- **PVGIS is a long-term monthly mean.** Single overcast days have almost no beam and no tracking gain; the lab weeks (late Nov, Dec) sit at the bottom of the curve.

## 7. Design rules for the control code

Target: **P_use <= 22.5 mW averaged over 24 h (0.54 Wh/day, 4.5 mA at 5 V)**. Hard limit: 29 mW (0.70 Wh/day), the December break-even.

1. **Sleep mode: light sleep, not deep sleep, and never a `delay()` loop.** Call `esp_light_sleep_start()` with a timer wake from the sampler (automatic light sleep is not available in this core build, see `firmware/README.md`). Awake at 80 MHz costs about 106 mW, which is 3.6 times the December budget on its own, so the MCU may be awake for at most about 5 % of the time. Budget used in (c)/(d): awake <= 0.25 s every 15 s (1.7 %).
2. **Sample interval: wake every 15 s, log every 60 s.** Set the INA226 to 1024 averages x 8.244 ms for both conversions (one result per 16.9 s, config 0x4FFF; the firmware now uses 64 averages, 0x47FF, one result per 1.06 s) so four reads per minute still cover the whole minute; the contract's one-minute mean (7.1) is kept. The INA226s stay in continuous mode: they are the measurement, and three of them cost only 0.08 Wh/day.
3. **Control loop only when it can matter.** Read the BPX65 arc and decide once per wake by day; at night (arc dark for 10 min) wake once a minute, skip the control step and leave the panel where the return move put it. The 5 Hz loop in the INTERFACE.md example must not be used as it is.
4. **Wi-Fi batching.** Keep the radio off between uploads (as built). At 15 min intervals the uploads cost 0.11 Wh/day, 16 % of the December budget; hourly (`upload every 60`) costs 0.03 Wh/day. Use 15 min while F8 stands; ask for F8 to be relaxed to 60 min for the winter months. Each upload must stay under 4.1 J: cut `kRadioLeadS` from 8 s once the real hotspot join time is known, and never leave the radio on waiting for NTP.
5. **Servo duty.** Move only when the pointing error exceeds the 5° dead band (about one move per 20 min in clear sun), one move per step, no hunting. One return move after dusk. Do not move when the arc reports no clear sun (state HLD). Keep the angle command inside the safe range (fault 7 if it is not). Budget: <= 2 J per small move and <= 106 J/day for all moves.
6. **Power-gate the servo: P-MOSFET in its 5 V feed, off except during a move.** Switch it on, wait for the servo to start, send the pulse, wait for the move to end, switch it off (about 1 s in all). Left on, the servo costs 1.2-4.8 Wh/day. A 3.3 V GPIO cannot switch off a P-MOSFET whose source is at 5 V on its own, and no Nano ESP32 pin is 5 V tolerant: drive the gate through a small N-MOSFET or NPN transistor with a pull-up to 5 V, so that the default state is off. Hold the GPIO state through light sleep (`gpio_hold_en`). If the servo stalls, switch the gate off and report fault 8 rather than wait for its 5 s protection. Gate only if bench test M9 shows that the panel stays put when the servo is not powered.
7. **Gate everything else.** BPX65 bias (and any angle potentiometer) from a GPIO that is high only in the wake window, or run the photodiodes unbiased. LEDs off (done); cut the green power LED for the outdoor unit. USB unplugged in every energy test.
8. **Stay awake only while the servo moves,** and call `telemetry_set_motor_running()` (the name in INTERFACE.md) around the move. With light sleep the INTERFACE.md rule "call at least once per second or the data link reports IDL after 5 s" has to be changed to match the 15 s wake: agree this with the data-link owner before coding.
9. **Check with the device itself.** `P_use` (field 3) over 24 h must come out at or below 0.54 Wh/day; above 0.70 Wh/day the December demonstration shows a net loss whatever the pointing accuracy.
