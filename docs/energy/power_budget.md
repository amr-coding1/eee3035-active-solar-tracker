# Power budget and break-even: can the panel pay for its own movement?

EEE3035 Group 8, Active Solar Tracker. Generated by `power_budget.py` on 19 Nov 2026; every number in this file comes from the script (`power_budget_numbers.json` holds them unrounded). **Nothing here has been measured on our hardware yet**: the values are datasheet figures, cited third-party measurements, or assumptions marked as such. Section 8 lists the bench measurements for 26 Nov that replace them.

## 1. Answer in brief

- **Break-even budget** (tracked minus fixed panel at the same tilt, PVGIS): the tracker may draw at most **0.70 Wh/day = 29 mW average in December**, 1.03 Wh/day (43 mW) in January, 1.46 Wh/day (61 mW) in November and 7.61 Wh/day (317 mW) in June. Year mean 4.44 Wh/day (185 mW).
- **(a) always awake, Wi-Fi on**: 5.02 Wh/day (209 mW). Net loss in 6 months (Jan, Feb, Mar, Oct, Nov, Dec); -4 % over the year.
- **(b) data link as built**: 3.11 Wh/day (130 mW). Net loss in 5 months (Jan, Feb, Oct, Nov, Dec); +9 % over the year.
- **(c) light sleep, 15 min uploads**: 0.53 Wh/day (22 mW). Net gain in every month; +26 % over the year.
- **(d) light sleep, hourly uploads, LED cut**: 0.33 Wh/day (14 mW). Net gain in every month; +27 % over the year.
- **Design target: P_use <= 22.5 mW average (0.54 Wh/day)** at INA226 #3. That is the December budget divided by 1.3, so the net gain stays positive in every month even if consumption is 30 % above the model.
- The motor is the smallest term if it is set up properly: 0.043 Wh/day for 240° of travel at 200 mA and 2°/s. The electronics that are on for 24 h decide the result, not the movement.

## 2. Method

1. `net gain = (E_tracked - E_fixed - E_use) / E_fixed` (contract 6.2). Net gain >= 0 exactly when `E_use <= E_tracked - E_fixed`. The right-hand side is the **break-even budget**; divided by 24 h it is the maximum average tracker power.
2. `E_tracked` and `E_fixed` are the PVGIS 5.3 monthly means already in the repository (docs/energy/pvgis_Ed_per_kWp_guildford.json): inclined-axis tracker and fixed panel at the same tilt (39°, south), 14 % system loss, scaled to one 5.4 W FIT0330 panel.
3. `E_use` is built bottom-up: for each operating mode, current of each part x rail voltage / converter efficiency x time in that mode. All scenario figures are **at the 5 V output of the Solar Power Manager**, which is where INA226 #3 has to sit to see the whole tracker and nothing else (see flag F1). 3.3 V loads go through the Nano's MP2322 buck converter (0.90 at normal load, 0.80 assumed at sleep currents).
4. The motor is added as a separate term (section 4) so the control-code author can see what each strategy costs.
5. Cross-check: with the repository's own `E_use` assumptions the script reproduces `docs/energy/net_gain_by_month.md` (section 5).

Flags (things the model had to assume):

- **F1, where INA226 #3 sits.** The firmware comments call 0x44 the "battery line" and report its bus voltage as V_bat. With the Solar Power Manager the battery line also carries the charging current, so a sensor there cannot separate what the tracker draws. For P_use the sensor has to be in the 5 V (or 3.3 V) output that feeds the tracker. Measured at the battery instead, every figure here is divided by the boost efficiency (0.86).
- **F2, Nano ESP32 supply.** Arduino recommends 6-21 V on VIN (the cheat sheet also says keep VIN within 5-18 V), so 5 V on VIN is below the recommended range, and VBUS is not an input (it only outputs 5 V when the board is powered from USB). Feed the power manager's 5 V USB output into the Nano's USB-C port, or 3.3 V into the 3V3 pin only as Arduino describes (solder jumper), which also removes the MP2322 loss.
- **F3, BPX65 arc.** The repository does not say how many photodiodes there are or how they are read; 5 diodes reverse-biased from 3V3 are assumed. Used in photovoltaic mode (no bias) they draw nothing.
- **F4, motor.** The part number, gear ratio, supply voltage, running current and panel speed are not in the repository. The 2°/s is the simulator default (`ast_sim.h`).
- **F5, panel-angle sensor and limit switches** are not in the hardware list and are not in the model. A 10 kΩ potentiometer across 3.3 V would add 0.33 mA (about 0.03 Wh/day): power it from a GPIO during the wake window.
- **F6, board sleep currents** come from forum users, not from Arduino. They vary by a factor of two.

### 4.1 Motor energy

Assumptions: travel 240°/day (±60° limits: 120° sweep at 15°/h over 8 h, then 120° back), 5 V, 200 mA running, 2°/s at the panel, start surge 1 A for 0.1 s, MCU awake and DRV8833 awake only while the motor runs. No holding current: the worm gear is self-locking.

| Move every | Moves/day | Step | Motor on (s/day) | Running (J) | Starts (J) | MCU + driver while moving (J) | **Total (Wh/day)** | Beam lost to pointing error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 min | 97 | 1.25° | 120 | 120 | 39 | 14 | **0.048** | 0.002 % |
| 10 min | 49 | 2.50° | 120 | 120 | 20 | 14 | **0.043** | 0.008 % |
| 15 min | 33 | 3.75° | 120 | 120 | 13 | 14 | **0.041** | 0.018 % |

The running time is fixed by the travel and the speed, not by the interval; only the number of starts changes. Range: 0.012 Wh/day (80 mA, 6°/s) to **0.68 Wh/day** (1 A, 0.5°/s), and the top of that range equals the whole December budget. Motor current and panel speed are therefore the two numbers to measure first (M7, M8). DRV8833 conduction loss at 200 mA is 14 mW while moving (already inside V x I). A 15 min step leaves at most 1.9° of error and costs under 0.02 % of the beam, so stepping more often buys nothing.

Left awake all day the DRV8833 alone costs 0.20 Wh/day (1.7 mA at 5 V), 29 % of the December budget and several times the motor itself.

- **PVGIS is a long-term monthly mean.** Single overcast days have almost no beam and no tracking gain; the lab weeks (late Nov, Dec) sit at the bottom of the curve.

## 7. Design rules for the control code

Target: **P_use <= 22.5 mW averaged over 24 h (0.54 Wh/day, 4.5 mA at 5 V)**. Hard limit: 29 mW (0.70 Wh/day), the December break-even.

1. **Sleep mode: light sleep, not deep sleep, and never a `delay()` loop.** Call `esp_light_sleep_start()` with a timer wake from the sampler (automatic light sleep is not available in this core build, see `firmware/README.md`). Awake at 80 MHz costs about 106 mW, which is 3.6 times the December budget on its own, so the MCU may be awake for at most about 5 % of the time. Budget used in (c)/(d): awake <= 0.25 s every 15 s (1.7 %).
2. **Sample interval: wake every 15 s, log every 60 s.** Set the INA226 to 1024 averages x 8.244 ms for both conversions (one result per 16.9 s, config 0x4FFF; the firmware now uses 64 averages, 0x47FF, one result per 1.06 s) so four reads per minute still cover the whole minute; the contract's one-minute mean (7.1) is kept. The INA226s stay in continuous mode: they are the measurement, and three of them cost only 0.08 Wh/day.
3. **Control loop only when it can matter.** Read the BPX65 arc and decide once per wake by day; at night (arc dark for 10 min) wake once a minute, skip the control step and leave the panel where the return move put it. The 5 Hz loop in the INTERFACE.md example must not be used as it is.
4. **Wi-Fi batching.** Keep the radio off between uploads (as built). At 15 min intervals the uploads cost 0.11 Wh/day, 16 % of the December budget; hourly (`upload every 60`) costs 0.03 Wh/day. Use 15 min while F8 stands; ask for F8 to be relaxed to 60 min for the winter months. Each upload must stay under 4.1 J: cut `kRadioLeadS` from 8 s once the real hotspot join time is known, and never leave the radio on waiting for NTP.
5. **Motor duty.** Move at most once every 10-15 min (dead-band 2.5-3.75°), one continuous move per step, no hunting: a second move within 5 min of the last is forbidden unless the error exceeds 5°. One return move after dusk. Do not move when the arc reports no clear sun (state HLD). Budget: motor-on time x motor power <= 360 J/day (0.1 Wh), i.e. <= 1.5 J per degree.
6. **DRV8833 nSLEEP on a GPIO, low except during a move.** Raise it, wait 1 ms, move, lower it. Left high it costs 0.20 Wh/day. Hold the GPIO state through light sleep (`gpio_hold_en`) or fit a pull-down.
7. **Gate everything else.** BPX65 bias (and any angle potentiometer) from a GPIO that is high only in the wake window, or run the photodiodes unbiased. LEDs off (done); cut the green power LED for the outdoor unit. USB unplugged in every energy test.
8. **Stay awake only while the motor runs,** and call `telemetry_set_motor_running()` around the move. With light sleep the INTERFACE.md rule "call at least once per second or the data link reports IDL after 5 s" has to be changed to match the 15 s wake: agree this with the data-link owner before coding.
9. **Check with the device itself.** `P_use` (field 3) over 24 h must come out at or below 0.54 Wh/day; above 0.70 Wh/day the December demonstration shows a net loss whatever the pointing accuracy.
